Interswitch East Africa

Running PI planning across five squads in East Africa

The problem

Five delivery squads were working on sprints that averaged 21 days, with outputs that were hard to measure. Cross-team dependencies surfaced late, work arrived at sprints under-refined, and problems reached leadership as escalations.

What I did

  • Facilitate Programme Increment (PI) planning for the region, and run Scrum of Scrums across five squads and six Scrum Masters.
  • Moved the squads to a fixed 14-day sprint cadence, with each sprint tied to measurable results and milestones.
  • Surface more than 20 cross-team dependencies at every PI planning event, so they are owned before they block delivery.
  • Use PI outcomes to tell clients when to expect each milestone, instead of committing dates before teams have planned.
  • Report predictability, velocity and delivery risk to EXCO through a dashboard leadership now uses.

Kenya Power & Lighting Company

Consolidating infrastructure across 32 sites

The problem

Infrastructure was spread across 32 regional sites. Keeping it running meant constant travel and duplicated running costs, and support depended on people being physically on site.

What I did

  • Proposed the consolidation and made the case for it: centralise infrastructure at the Nairobi HQ and disaster recovery sites and decommission regional infrastructure.
  • Ran the programme under PRINCE2: a steering committee, a documented business case, stage gates and a live risk and issues register.
  • Managed three external vendors, with payments tied to milestone acceptance and formal change control to stop scope creep eroding the budget.
  • Coordinated IT, operations, finance, procurement and regional field teams, and trained around 120 staff and field staff after rollout.

The result

Delivered within budget over 14 months. Six months after go-live, operations were running the estate independently with stable performance.

When delivery gets hard

Situations every delivery leader meets, and how I handled them. Client and vendor names are withheld.

A vendor missed its delivery date

Situation
A vendor delivered late, putting the agreed go-live at risk.
What I did
Escalated on the contract and held the vendor to its milestones and a recovery plan. Went to the client early and agreed a new go-live date, instead of letting the original one slip.
Result
Went live on the revised date the client had agreed to.

A squad that kept missing its commitments

Situation
One squad was regularly missing its sprint commitments.
What I did
Had the squad commit to its real capacity instead of over-committing at sprint planning, and made dependencies and blockers a check before each sprint rather than a surprise during it.
Result
The squad started meeting its sprint commitments.

New work pushed in mid-PI

Situation
Leadership asked for new work in the middle of a PI, on top of what the squads had already committed to.
What I did
Pushed back at EXCO to protect the PI commitments. Made the trade-off explicit: the new work had to move to the next PI or replace something already committed.
Result
PI commitments held. The new work went into the next PI or was traded against existing objectives.

Holding the line close to release

Situation
Late change requests arrived close to release, and client milestones were at risk of being promised before teams had planned them.
What I did
Refused late changes that would put a release at risk, and introduced a Definition of Ready so work only enters a sprint when it is ready. When a client milestone had to move, I told the client and managed that conversation directly.
Result
Better-refined work reaching the squads, and client milestone dates set from PI planning rather than guessed.

How I run a PMO

One source of truthEvery project reports the same way, so leadership reads one portfolio view instead of reconciling spreadsheets.
Gates that fit the riskStage gates scale with a project's value and exposure. Small changes move fast; large programmes earn each next stage.
Clear decision rightsEveryone knows what a PM can decide, what goes to the sponsor and what goes to the steering committee.
Capacity before commitmentNew work is agreed against the people actually available, so the portfolio stops promising more than it can deliver.
Vendors paid on outcomesPayments tie to accepted milestones, and change control protects the budget.
Measured on outcome, not outputA project closes when its benefit is tracked and support owns it, after a short pilot monitoring window.
